# Liability (everything other than the rental car)

Liability covers injury to other people and damage to their property when the driver is at fault. **Credit cards never cover liability.**

## Who covers whom

- A US personal auto policy's liability generally **follows the driver**. It covers the named insured, their resident spouse, and resident relatives while driving a non-owned auto (including a rental) anywhere in the policy territory.
- Each friend or other driver relies on **their own** liability policy. Ask for each driver's limits (e.g. 100/300/100 = $100k per person bodily injury / $300k per accident / $100k property damage).
- A driver with **no auto policy** has no personal liability coverage. They need the rental company's supplemental liability, a **non-owner auto policy**, or must not drive.
- **Rental company's own liability.** In some states the rental company is required to (or does) provide state-minimum liability on its cars, often secondary to the driver's own policy. State minimums are low (often $25k–$50k per person). Don't count on it for the $300k test.
- **Supplemental liability (SLI / LIS / ALI)** sold at the counter: excess liability up to a stated limit. Major US companies' published limits (last checked 2026-10, verify): about **$300,000** combined single limit at Hertz, Enterprise, National and Alamo, and **$500,000** at Avis and Budget (some locations or vehicle types differ). The supplement is usually excess over the rental company's own state-minimum liability and may duplicate or change how the driver's own policy applies. **Use the limit actually quoted on the agreement or the company's website.** Rental supplemental liability alone **rarely meets the $1M recommendation**. Mention that an umbrella or higher personal limits are the only way to get there for most renters. Abroad, most countries require the rental company to include liability in the price, and it is usually the main coverage. See `international.md` for limits by region.
- **Umbrella**: sits on top of the auto liability. See `umbrella.md`.

## The $300k test (per driver)

Total liability that applies to the driver = their own auto policy limit (use the **per-accident bodily injury** limit for the test, and note the property damage limit) + any umbrella that applies + any SLI bought.

- **< $300k** → fails. Fix: buy SLI for the trip; long term, raise limits or add an umbrella.
- **$300k to $999k** → passes, with "recommended: raise to $1M+."
- **≥ $1M** → passes.

Note a split-limit policy with a low per-person limit (e.g. 50/300) as a weakness even if it passes.

## No-fault states and PIP

In no-fault states each person's own Personal Injury Protection (PIP) pays their medical costs first, regardless of fault. Out-of-state drivers can run into trouble if they lack PIP that meets the state's minimum.

**What to check:**
1. Is the rental state a no-fault or PIP-required state? (table below, then verify)
2. Does each driver's policy have an **out-of-state coverage** clause? Standard ISO wording automatically raises limits and adds the coverage types (including PIP) required by the state where an accident occurs, for compulsory financial-responsibility or no-fault laws. Quote it if present.
3. If a driver lacks the clause or has no policy, flag the risk and suggest the rental company's personal accident insurance (PAI) only as a partial medical stopgap (it's not PIP and doesn't satisfy the law), and the real fixes: SLI, a non-owner policy, or not driving.

### No-fault / PIP states (verify; last checked 2026-10)

These figures change. When the rental state is on this list, confirm the current requirement with the state insurance department or statute and cite it.

| State | Type | Required PIP minimum (approx.) |
|---|---|---|
| Florida | No-fault | $10,000. Repeal bills (SB 522 / HB 769) died in the 2026 session, so PIP is still required. Some websites wrongly say PIP ended July 1, 2026. Recheck if the rental is after a later session. |
| Hawaii | No-fault | $10,000 |
| Kansas | No-fault | $4,500 medical, plus other benefits |
| Kentucky | Choice no-fault | $10,000. Drivers can reject parts of the no-fault system, and sources disagree on whether PIP itself can be rejected; verify. |
| Massachusetts | No-fault | $8,000 |
| Michigan | No-fault | Tiered choices (up to unlimited) |
| Minnesota | No-fault | $40,000 ($20k medical + $20k non-medical) |
| New Jersey | Choice no-fault | $15,000 basic (standard policies offer much more). NJ minimums were scheduled to rise on 2026-01-01; verify. |
| New York | No-fault | $50,000 |
| North Dakota | No-fault | $30,000 |
| Pennsylvania | Choice no-fault | $5,000 first-party medical benefits |
| Utah | No-fault | $3,000 |
| Delaware | At-fault with required PIP | $15,000 per person / $30,000 per accident |
| Oregon | At-fault with required PIP | $15,000 per person |
| Maryland | At-fault, PIP must be offered | $2,500, can be waived. A Maryland waiver may not hold in Delaware, where courts have applied Delaware's minimum through the policy's out-of-state clause. |

All other states are at-fault (tort) states. Some require PIP or medical payments to be offered but allow rejecting them.

## Rental counter products, translated

| Counter name | What it is |
|---|---|
| CDW / LDW | Damage waiver for the rental car (not liability) |
| SLI / LIS / ALI / EP | Supplemental / extended liability |
| PAI | Personal accident insurance: medical and death benefits for the driver and passengers |
| PEC / PEP | Personal effects coverage: belongings in the car |
