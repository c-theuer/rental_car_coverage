# Other risks that can void or limit coverage

Check each of these during intake and analysis. Any that applies goes in the report.

## Car-sharing apps (Turo, Getaround, and similar)

Peer-to-peer car sharing is **not** a rental from a rental company, and coverage that works for Hertz or Enterprise often doesn't apply:

- **Credit cards:** most card rental benefits exclude it. Amex's terms explicitly exclude "vehicle sharing or peer-to-peer arrangements which allow individual owners to rent personal vehicles." Check the user's guide, but expect no card coverage.
- **Personal auto policy:** many insurers exclude car-sharing rentals, or treat them differently from rental company cars. Check the policy form for "car sharing," "peer-to-peer," or "personal vehicle sharing program" wording, or call the insurer.
- **Result:** the user usually depends on the **app's own protection plan** for damage, and on the app's included liability (check its limit against the $300k test). Read the plan's deductible and what it excludes.

## Breaking the rental agreement

Breaking the agreement's terms usually voids the rental company's waiver **and** the card benefit, and can give the personal insurer grounds to deny. Beyond territory (see `international.md`) and unlisted drivers, common violations:

- Off-road or unpaved-road driving (card guides commonly exclude off-road use)
- Towing or pushing anything
- Driving under the influence of alcohol or drugs
- Using the car for hire: ride-share (Uber, Lyft), delivery apps, or carrying paying passengers
- A driver without a valid license, or with a suspended one
- Racing, speed tests, or reckless driving
- Exceeding passenger or weight limits

Remind the user to read the agreement's "prohibited use" section.

## Using your own policy can raise your premium

A damage or liability claim on the personal auto policy is a claim on the user's record, even if the card reimburses the deductible. It can raise premiums or, after several claims, lead to non-renewal. This affects the recommendation:

- A card with **primary** coverage pays first and keeps the claim off the personal policy. (This only matters for someone who has a personal policy. Without one, a secondary card already pays first.)
- With a secondary card, buying the rental company's CDW/LDW, or a standalone primary product (below), may be worth it for someone who wants to avoid a claim on their policy.

State this trade-off in the report whenever the personal policy would be the first payer.

## Expensive cars and EVs

- Ask for the make and model. Treat **EVs** (Tesla, Polestar, Rivian, etc.) and luxury cars as high-value: repairs are expensive, and many are worth more than common caps.
- Compare the car's value to:
  - the card's maximum (e.g. $75,000 on Chase Sapphire Reserve)
  - the personal policy's rental limit (which may be tied to the user's own car's value; see `damage.md`)
  - the card's excluded-vehicle list (expensive, exotic and some luxury models are often excluded)
- If the car's value exceeds what's covered, that's a **damage gap**.

## Injuries to you and your passengers

The "fully covered" test checks damage and liability to others. Also report on:

- **Uninsured/underinsured motorist (UM/UIM)** coverage. It follows the driver on their personal policy. Rental company supplemental liability includes little or none (for example, Hertz's supplement includes no UM/UIM in California and caps it elsewhere). A driver with no personal policy usually has no UM/UIM.
- **Medical payments / PIP** on the driver's own policy.
- **Passengers** (e.g. friends): their injuries are covered by the at-fault driver's liability, their own PIP or medical payments coverage, or their health insurance. The rental company's personal accident insurance (PAI) mostly duplicates health insurance.

These don't change the pass/fail verdict, but include them under "Also consider."

## Standalone rental damage products

Short-term fixes when the card and personal policy fall short:

- **Amex Premium Car Rental Protection**: enroll an Amex card, pay a flat fee per rental (reported $19.95 Basic / $24.95 Plus, less for Florida and California residents), and get primary damage and theft coverage (reported up to $75,000 Basic / $100,000 Plus) for rentals of up to about 42 days. It excludes Australia, Ireland, Israel, Italy, Jamaica and New Zealand. Verify the current price, limits, and primary status on Amex's site.
- **Third-party rental damage insurance** sold by booking sites or insurers: usually **reimbursement**. The rental company charges the renter first, and the renter files a claim afterward. Abroad, the counter may still require its own CDW or put a large hold on the card.
- **Rental company CDW/LDW**: simplest, priciest, and voids the card benefit.

## Non-owner auto policies

A non-owner policy gives **liability** (and sometimes UM/UIM and medical payments) to someone who doesn't own a car. It usually does **not** cover damage to the rental car. When recommending one, pair it with a card benefit, a standalone damage product, or the counter's CDW.

## Booking and payment

Card coverage usually requires the **entire** rental to be charged to the eligible card (or paid with that card's points):

- **Prepaid bookings** through Expedia or other sites: the card used on the booking site must be the eligible card. Check the guide for prepaid or third-party bookings.
- **Split payment**, gift cards, vouchers, or a different card at the counter can void coverage.
- Free rentals from loyalty points: check whether the guide covers them.

## Also consider (mention when relevant)

- **Belongings in the car**: usually covered by the user's homeowners or renters insurance (off-premises coverage, subject to the deductible), not auto insurance. The rental company's personal effects coverage often duplicates it.
- **Charges no policy covers**: lost keys, lockouts, tolls and toll-device fees, tickets, cleaning, smoking or pet fees, fuel.
- **State rules on rental damage**: some states regulate what rental companies can charge renters for damage and how damage waivers are sold or priced (for example New York, California, Nevada). Check the state if a dispute arises.
- **Rentals while your car is in the shop**: the policy's rental reimbursement coverage pays the rental bill, but it's separate from coverage for damage to the rental.
- **Drivers under 25**: extra fees, and some rental companies, cards or vehicle classes restrict them.
- **Renting for someone else**: if the cardholder rents but won't drive, check that the card covers authorized drivers when the cardholder isn't driving, and that the actual drivers are listed and covered.
