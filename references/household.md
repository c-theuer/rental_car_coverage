# Households, multiple drivers, and authorized users

## Personal auto policy with several drivers

Standard ISO personal auto policy definitions (verify the user's policy; many insurers modify these):

- **"You" / "your"**: the named insured on the declarations **and the named insured's spouse, if a resident of the same household**. Some policies extend this to a registered domestic partner or civil union partner; check. A spouse who moves out of the household usually keeps "you" status only for a limited period (often 90 days) or until the end of the policy term.
- **"Family member"**: a person related by blood, marriage, or adoption who is a **resident of your household**, including a ward or foster child.

Coverage for damage to a non-owned (rental) car applies to "you" and "family members." So:

| Driver | Usually covered on a rental under this policy? |
|---|---|
| Named insured | Yes |
| Spouse living in the household | Yes (counts as "you") |
| Relative living in the household (e.g. a child at home) | Yes ("family member") |
| Domestic partner who isn't a spouse | **Check the policy.** Often not, unless named on the declarations or the policy defines them in. |
| Other **listed** driver who isn't a resident relative (roommate, nanny, adult child living elsewhere) | **Check.** Being listed for *your* cars doesn't automatically extend to non-owned cars. |
| College student away at school | Often still a resident relative; check the policy's residency wording. |
| **Named excluded driver** | **No coverage at all**, on any car. Never let an excluded driver drive the rental. |
| Unrelated friend | No, under the renter's policy. Look to the friend's own policy and the card. |

Liability works the same way: the renter's policy liability usually covers "you" and "family members" driving a non-owned auto. Others rely on their own policy.

## Credit card authorized users (AU)

- An authorized user gets a card on the primary cardholder's account. Whether an AU is an **eligible person** for the rental benefit depends on the Guide to Benefits; most premium cards include AUs, some don't. Check the definition of "cardholder" / "eligible person."
- The benefit usually applies only when **the renter named on the rental agreement is the eligible cardholder** and **the full rental is paid with that cardholder's card**.
- Other drivers listed on the agreement are covered as **authorized drivers** (if the guide extends coverage to them, as most do).
- Adding an AU may cost money (some premium cards charge an annual AU fee). Mention the fee if known; tell the user to check.

## Married couple: one auto policy, one card account

Assume Spouse A is the primary cardholder and named insured, and Spouse B is the spouse living in the same household.

| Situation | Personal policy (damage) | Card (damage + fees) | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| A rents, pays with A's card, B listed as additional driver | Both covered (both are "you") | A covered as renter; B covered as authorized driver | **Both pass** |
| B rents in B's name, pays with **A's card**, B **not** an AU | B covered | **Likely fails**: the renter isn't a cardholder, and the card wasn't B's | **Gap**: deductible and loss of use likely uncovered. Prompt the user. |
| B is an AU, rents in B's name, pays with **B's AU card** | B covered | B covered as eligible cardholder (if the guide covers AUs) | **Pass** |
| B is an AU, rents, pays with A's physical card | B covered | Check: the card used wasn't B's. Many guides would still fail this. | Treat as **unverified/gap** and recommend B use their own card |
| Either spouse drives but isn't on the agreement | **No damage coverage** | **No** | **Fail**: never do this |

**Prompt the user** whenever Spouse B would be the renter without being an eligible cardholder on the paying card. Offer:
1. A rents and lists B as an additional driver (simplest, no fee), or
2. Add B as an AU (check the fee and that AUs are eligible), and B rents and pays with B's own AU card.

## Adding a spouse as an additional driver

Many rental companies don't charge to add a spouse or domestic partner (some company policies and some state laws require this, e.g. California). Always list the spouse on the agreement at the counter. Fees for other additional drivers vary.

## Two separate households (e.g. friends renting together)

Each friend:
- is not covered by the renter's personal policy (unless a resident relative),
- may be covered by **their own** policy's non-owned auto damage coverage if they are on the agreement (check their policy form and declarations: do they carry collision?),
- is covered by the renter's card as an authorized driver only if the guide extends coverage to authorized drivers on the agreement,
- relies on **their own** liability coverage (see `liability.md`).
