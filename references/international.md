# Renting outside the 50 states

## Rule: confirm North American coverage, never assume it

For **any** rental or driving outside the 50 states in North America (Canada, Mexico, Puerto Rico, the US Virgin Islands, Guam and other US territories, the Caribbean, Central America, Bermuda, Greenland), **do not assume** the personal auto policy applies. Coverage must be confirmed by one of these:

1. **The policy documents.** Quote the "policy territory" (or "where coverage applies") clause from the user's actual policy form, plus any endorsement that changes it. The standard ISO form reads roughly "the United States of America, its territories or possessions; Puerto Rico; or Canada," but insurers and state forms differ, so the user's own wording controls.
2. **A call to the insurer.** If the documents are missing or unclear, tell the user to call and ask, for each place on the route: Does my **liability** apply? Does my **collision/comprehensive** apply to a **rental** there? Are there limits on trip length or distance? Can you send written confirmation or proof of insurance I can carry? Tell them to note the date and the representative's name, or get it by email.

Until one of these confirms it, mark the personal policy as **⚠️ unverified** for that place in the report. Don't count it toward the damage or $300k liability tests.

## Canada

- Confirm per the rule above. Many US policies include Canada, but not all.
- If covered, ask the insurer for a Canadian proof of insurance card (the "pink card") or equivalent to carry.
- Provinces have their own minimums and some have no-fault benefits. Check whether the policy's out-of-state clause applies to provinces.
- Check the card guide for Canada.

## US territories (Puerto Rico, USVI, Guam, etc.)

- Confirm per the rule above.
- Puerto Rico has its own compulsory insurance system (often included in the vehicle's registration). The rental company handles this for the car; the driver's liability is still the issue.
- Check the card guide's country/territory rules.

## Mexico

- **US auto liability is not recognized by Mexican law.** Mexican-issued third-party liability insurance is **legally required**. Rental companies in Mexico include or require it. Without it, a driver in an accident can be detained.
- Some US insurers offer a limited Mexico endorsement for **physical damage only** (never liability). Typical terms: accidents within 25–75 miles of the border, trips of 10 days or less, often excess over other insurance. Many insurers don't offer it at all, and it usually applies to the insured's own car, not a car rented in Mexico. Confirm per the rule above. Otherwise there is no personal-policy damage coverage in Mexico.
- Many card guides cover Mexico for damage; some exclude it or require declining specific products. Verify. Cards don't cover the Mexican liability requirement.
- For the $300k test: check the limit of the Mexican liability included or sold with the rental. It is often well under US levels. A **true umbrella** with worldwide coverage may sit on top (see `umbrella.md`).

## Caribbean, Central America, and other North American countries

- Treat like "outside North America" below unless the policy documents or the insurer confirm otherwise. Standard US policy territories don't include them.
- Check the card guide: Jamaica is a common exclusion.

## Countries where credit cards commonly can't insure (last checked 2026-10)

Exclusions depend on the **card network and the specific card**. Always check the guide for the user's card, and when the country is on any list below, tell the user to get a **letter of coverage** from the benefit administrator naming the country. Rental desks in these countries often refuse to let a renter decline their CDW without one, or at all.

| Network / issuer | Commonly excluded (verify) |
|---|---|
| Visa (standard Auto Rental CDW) | Israel, Jamaica, Republic of Ireland, Northern Ireland |
| Mastercard | Israel, Jamaica, Republic of Ireland, Northern Ireland; plus anywhere coverage is prohibited by local law |
| American Express (Car Rental Loss & Damage) | Australia, Italy, New Zealand, and OFAC-sanctioned countries. Older Amex guides also excluded Ireland, Israel, and Jamaica. |
| Some issuers (e.g. Chase Sapphire Preferred/Reserve, many Citi cards) | Have reportedly dropped country exclusions. Confirm in the current guide. |
| All cards | OFAC-sanctioned countries; countries where local law prohibits the coverage or requires buying local CDW |

Related issues:
- **Italy**: theft protection is required by law, and rental companies in practice include CDW in the price too (sources disagree on whether CDW itself is legally mandatory). Either way, the renter **can't decline** them, which can block the card's "decline the CDW" condition even on cards that don't exclude Italy. The included protections usually carry a high deductible. Check whether the card will cover that deductible when the waiver couldn't be declined, and get it in writing.
- **Ireland / Northern Ireland**: many rental companies accept only certain cards for declining CDW and may require the letter of coverage.
- Many guides word the exclusion as rentals **rented in** or **originating in** a country. A car rented in an allowed country and driven into an excluded country is a gray area. Treat it as **unverified** and tell the user to get written confirmation from the benefit administrator.

## Cross-border driving: where it's rented vs where it's driven

Always ask both **where the car will be rented** and **every country (or US state/Mexico/Canada) it will be driven in**, including ferries. Then check all three layers:

1. **Rental agreement territory.** Rental companies restrict where their cars can go. Typical rules: a cross-border fee and advance notice are required; some countries are banned entirely (commonly parts of Eastern Europe and the Balkans, or luxury brands restricted to a few countries); ferries to islands or other countries (e.g. Great Britain to Ireland, mainland to Corsica/Sardinia/Sicily) may need permission; most US rentals forbid driving into Mexico except special arrangements, and Canada usually requires notice. **Violating the rental agreement's territory terms voids the agreement's protections and almost always leaves the renter with no damage coverage** from the card, the personal policy, or the rental company's own CDW. Theft or damage in a forbidden country becomes the renter's full responsibility.
2. **Card territory.** Each country on the route must be covered by the card (see table above), and the rental length limit must hold.
3. **Liability.** The included and supplemental liability bought from the rental company must apply in every country on the route (in Europe, check the Green Card / cross-border insurance). For US policies, every place on the route must be inside the policy territory, **confirmed** from the policy documents or by the insurer, never assumed.

**Example: rented in France, driven into Italy.** Usually allowed by major French rental companies with notice and possibly a fee, but it must be declared and written on the agreement. If the card excludes Italy (e.g. Amex), damage in Italy may not be covered even though the car was rented in France. Get written confirmation from the benefit administrator, or buy the rental company's CDW. Also confirm the rental's liability coverage extends to Italy.

Flag every leg of the route that fails or is unverified on any layer.

## Liability included by law in most other countries

In most countries outside the US, the law makes the **vehicle owner** carry compulsory third-party liability insurance, so the **rental company provides it and it's included in the rental price**. In the US, by contrast, much of the responsibility falls on the driver's own policy. What the included cover means for the $300k test:

| Where | Included liability | For the test |
|---|---|---|
| EU / EEA | Compulsory, must be included in the rental price and valid across the EU. Minimums are at least **€6.45M per accident for injury** (or €1.3M per injured person) and **€1.3M per accident for property damage**; some countries require more or unlimited injury cover. Often shown as "TPL," "TPI," or "civil liability." | Usually **passes** the $300k test, and often the $1M recommendation. Confirm the limit on the rental agreement. |
| UK | Compulsory and included; injury cover is unlimited by law and property cover is high. | Usually passes. Confirm. |
| Australia | Compulsory third party (CTP) covers **injury only**, not damage to other people's property. Property damage depends on the rental company's terms, and the renter may be charged up to the excess. | **Check** the rental terms for third-party property damage and its limit. |
| New Zealand | Injuries are covered by the government no-fault ACC scheme. Property damage to others depends on the rental company's terms. | **Check** the rental terms for third-party property damage. |
| Mexico | Mexican liability is legally required and included or required by the rental company. Limits vary widely and are often low. | **Check** the limit; often fails the $300k test without supplemental cover. |
| Elsewhere | Usually compulsory and included at the local legal minimum, which can be very low. | **Check** the limit on the rental agreement. |

Rules:
- Always read the **included liability limit** from the rental agreement or the company's terms for that country. Don't assume "included" means "enough."
- If the included limit is below $300k, the driver **fails** the liability test unless supplemental liability bought from the rental company or a true umbrella makes up the difference.
- On a cross-border route, confirm the included liability is valid in every country (in Europe, the Green Card system covers this).

## Everywhere else (outside North America)

- The personal auto policy **does not follow** the user. No damage or liability coverage from it.
- **Damage**: the card benefit may be **primary** abroad. Check the guide for excluded countries (see the table above), rental length (often 31 days), and vehicle types. In some countries the rental rate includes mandatory CDW with a high deductible; check whether the card covers that deductible.
- **Liability**: see "Liability included by law in most other countries" above. Read the included limit, and add supplemental liability or rely on a true umbrella if it's under $300k.
- **Umbrella**: a **true umbrella** with worldwide territory can cover liability above what's included; a follow-form excess policy generally can't (no underlying auto policy applies abroad). Check the umbrella's "retained limit" / drop-down wording: if the policy requires underlying auto limits that don't exist abroad, the user may be responsible for that layer.
- An International Driving Permit may be required (e.g. Japan, Italy, and others). Mention it.
