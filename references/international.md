# Renting outside the 50 states

First, find the **policy territory** in the personal auto policy form. Standard ISO wording: the United States, its territories or possessions, Puerto Rico, and Canada. Many insurers use this; some differ. Always tell the user to **call their insurer and confirm** before an out-of-country trip, and to ask for proof of insurance to carry.

## Canada

- Usually inside the policy territory: the user's US liability and damage coverage typically apply.
- Confirm with the insurer. Carry the insurance ID card; ask the insurer whether they issue a Canadian proof of insurance card.
- Canadian provinces have their own minimums and some have no-fault systems. The out-of-state/province clause usually conforms limits; verify.
- Check the credit card guide for Canada (usually covered).

## US territories (Puerto Rico, USVI, Guam, etc.)

- Often inside the policy territory, but **confirm with the insurer**. Some insurers or state-specific policy forms differ.
- Puerto Rico has its own compulsory insurance system (often included in the vehicle's registration). The rental company handles this for the car; the driver's liability is still the issue.
- Check the card guide's country/territory rules.

## Mexico

- **US auto liability is not recognized by Mexican law.** Mexican-issued third-party liability insurance is **legally required**. Rental companies in Mexico include or require it. Without it, a driver in an accident can be detained.
- Some US policies extend **physical damage** (not liability) to Mexico within a set distance of the border (e.g. 25 or 75 miles) by endorsement. Check the policy. Otherwise assume no personal-policy coverage.
- Many card guides cover Mexico for damage; some exclude it or require declining specific products. Verify. Cards don't cover the Mexican liability requirement.
- For the $300k test: Mexican rental liability limits are often well under US levels. A **true umbrella** with worldwide coverage may sit on top (see `umbrella.md`).

## Everywhere else (outside North America)

- The personal auto policy **does not follow** the user. No damage or liability coverage from it.
- **Damage**: the card benefit may be **primary** abroad. Check the guide for excluded countries (commonly Israel, Jamaica, Republic of Ireland, Northern Ireland, and sometimes others), rental length (often 31 days), and vehicle types. In some countries the rental rate includes mandatory CDW with a high deductible; check whether the card covers that deductible.
- **Liability**: basic liability is usually included in the rental price by law, at local minimum levels. Supplemental liability sold abroad often caps around €/$300k or less. Compare to the $300k test and the $1M recommendation.
- **Umbrella**: a **true umbrella** with worldwide territory can cover liability above what's included; a follow-form excess policy generally can't (no underlying auto policy applies abroad). Check the umbrella's "retained limit" / drop-down wording: if the policy requires underlying auto limits that don't exist abroad, the user may be responsible for that layer.
- An International Driving Permit may be required (e.g. Japan, Italy, and others). Mention it.
